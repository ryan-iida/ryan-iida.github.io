Status: Ongoing

For: Qi Li Group

Weather applications in cities like New York City do not illustrate a complete picture; the climate experienced at the street level varies greatly from street to street due to variations in the shade produced by tree canopy cover and building geometry on a street by street basis. The purpose of this project, Improving Thermal Comfort for Cyclists and Pedestrians by Considering Tree Shading in Routing, is to create an accessible navigation application that provides pedestrians and cyclists with directions to their destinations in New York City along preferable routes with the most shade amidst varying weather conditions.

This project mainly utilized the python libraries osmnx and pybdshadow in conjunction with building data provided by the City of New York and New York City tree canopy data provided by Professor Daniel Katz to inform a navigation routing algorithm that prefers cycling routes that are more heavily shaded. This process is accomplished by simulating building shadows and overlaying tree canopy data over street networks to modify model edge weights to make more heavily shaded streets preferable to the algorithm. The clarity of these shadows were then modified to allow for faster shadow calculation as edge weights were precalculated and tabulated in advance across constant time intervals for faster routing on a user’s end. While it is evident that this routing algorithm produces routes different from a simple time-based algorithm, the extent to which the routes calculated are more thermally-comfortable than typical routes has yet to be measured.

This project is funded by the Aizen Climate Research Scholars Fund.
