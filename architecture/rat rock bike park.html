Completed: June 2024

For: Columbia GSAPP Intro to Architecture

Site: Rat Rock, Morningside Heights

Sat between two apartment buildings at the border of Morningside Heights and West Harlem is Rat Rock—a fragment of schist that pierces through the city. It begs the question: what else is underneath us? Hundreds of miles of tunnels, pipes, and cables run underneath every sidewalk, and maintaining this infrastructure an incredibly intensive process; massive amounts of energy and labor is required to keep New York City functioning.

In designing a proposal for an infill site that utilizes Rat Rock as a locus of power, I chose cyclists as the protagonists of the space as they work to offset the harms of The City’s maintenance. Not a single protected bike lane is present in West Harlem’s busy streets, endangering cyclists and furthering the moribund cycle of vehicular traffic. Furthermore, the streets’ lack of trees further contribute to the hot discomfort of commuting on foot or by bike.

Through researching the role of bike co-ops and cycling activist spaces, I worked to create a democratic space where cyclists and the traffic vulnerable could control their surroundings with modular parts. Bright colors and low ceiling heights wel- come pedestrians and cyclists, creating a space that can only be enjoyed when traveling at a speed slower than a car. The space also features an autonomous climate system that supports vining plants in order to promote shading and natural ventilation.
